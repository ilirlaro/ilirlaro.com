Taking Litecoin into mainstream culture through UFC 232

How I helped connect Litecoin Foundation with UFC and supported one of the earliest examples of a major cryptocurrency brand entering mainstream combat sports.

Crypto awareness was growing, but much of the conversation was still happening inside crypto

When I joined the Litecoin Foundation, the industry was still relatively early in its development. Crypto had already experienced a major cycle of mainstream attention, but much of the conversation around individual assets still happened inside exchanges, specialist media and online communities that were already familiar with the category. If Litecoin was going to grow beyond that audience, awareness needed to extend into places where people were not already spending their time thinking about blockchain.

My role at the Foundation sat close to that adoption problem. I was active across social media, community communications and educational content, helping explain Litecoin to a wider audience and supporting initiatives intended to increase awareness and use. At the same time, my earlier work in the Endeavor ecosystem had given me exposure to the sports and streaming world around UFC. That overlap created an opportunity to connect two industries that, at the time, rarely appeared together.

Creating the bridge between UFC and Litecoin Foundation

The opportunity began through direct conversations on the UFC side. I engaged with people connected to the organisation, explained Litecoin, the role of the Foundation and what we were trying to achieve around broader adoption, and then helped introduce the opportunity back into Litecoin Foundation. My contribution was therefore at the front of the relationship: recognising that the two audiences could make sense together, opening the conversation and helping both sides understand why a partnership was worth exploring.

I would not describe myself as the person who single-handedly executed the sponsorship. I was still early in my career and the Foundation had project coordination and other stakeholders responsible for the operational delivery, sponsorship assets and media requirements once the partnership moved forward. The useful part of my role was helping create the bridge and then supporting the communications and education around it as the activation developed.

Why UFC made sense for Litecoin

UFC offered access to something crypto marketing often struggled to reach at the time: a large mainstream audience with a strong community culture and global distribution. The value was not simply putting a logo in front of a large number of people. Combat sports audiences were highly engaged and digitally active, while the event itself reached well beyond people who would normally follow cryptocurrency news.

That made UFC 232 particularly interesting as an awareness opportunity. Instead of competing for attention inside existing crypto channels, Litecoin could appear in the physical environment of a major sporting event alongside established consumer brands. The partnership positioned Litecoin as the Official Cryptocurrency Partner of UFC 232, with the Litecoin brand visible on the Octagon canvas during the event.

A partnership executed as a wider team effort

Once the relationship moved forward, the activation became a broader Foundation effort. Project management, sponsorship assets and event execution involved the relevant internal and external stakeholders, while my focus remained closer to communications, community and education. That distinction is important because the case study is not about claiming ownership over work completed by the wider team; it is about the role I played in originating the connection and helping turn the partnership into something the Litecoin community and a broader audience could understand.

Using the moment to educate, not only announce

A large part of my work at Litecoin Foundation involved producing educational content and staying active across the community. The UFC partnership created a mainstream moment around which that work could be concentrated. I supported social communications, wrote educational material and helped explain why a sports partnership mattered for a cryptocurrency whose broader goal was increasing awareness and adoption.

I also authored the post-sponsorship report published after UFC 232. That was important because I did not want the partnership to exist only as an announcement. The report documented the response around the activation and created a public record of what happened after Litecoin entered the Octagon. It also reflected a habit that has followed me through later roles: treating launch communications and measurement as parts of the same process rather than considering the work complete once something has gone live.

What the partnership achieved

The clearest impact was brand awareness outside the usual crypto environment. Public reporting around the activation cited more than 40 million Twitter impressions for “Litecoin UFC” during the period around UFC 232. More broadly, the partnership generated coverage across crypto and combat-sports media while putting Litecoin branding directly into a globally distributed sporting event.

I would not attribute Litecoin's market price or market-cap position to the UFC partnership. Crypto markets are influenced by too many variables for that to be credible, particularly during a period that also included wider discussion around Litecoin adoption, the upcoming halving and protocol development. The more defensible result was that Litecoin appeared in a mainstream cultural environment that would have been difficult to reach through conventional crypto channels alone.

Why the project mattered in my career

This project happened early in my career, which is one reason it remains particularly important to me. I had not yet spent years inside large global marketing organisations or built formal go-to-market operating models. What I had was familiarity with two different industries, enough curiosity to recognise that they could fit together and the willingness to start the conversation.

The project taught me that adoption is not only a technology problem. It is also a distribution, education and cultural problem.

That idea became increasingly important as my career progressed. At Binance, Uphold and Crypto.com, the scale and complexity of the problems became much greater, but the underlying question was often similar: how do you take something technically complex, make it understandable, place it in front of the right audience and create enough trust and relevance for people to engage with it?

The Litecoin x UFC 232 partnership was one of the earliest times I saw that process work in practice. It also showed me that some of the most valuable opportunities come from understanding two different worlds well enough to see the connection before it becomes obvious to everybody else.
